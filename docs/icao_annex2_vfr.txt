# This is the Annex 2 to the ICAO Convention on International Civil Aviation.
# This Annex sets out the Rules of the Air governing the operation of aircraft in flight and on the ground.
# This Document contains Chapter 4 - Visual Flight Rules (PARAPHRASED).

# VFR Weather Assistant FAQ\nHere you can find applicable visual flight rules that are defined in the
# International Standards for Rules of the Air:

4.1 Unless operating as a special VFR flight, pilots flying under visual flight rules must maintain weather visibility
and cloud separation minimums that meet or exceed those outlined in the following visual meteorological conditions
(VMC) minima based on altitude bands and airspace classes. For altitudes at and above 3,050 meters (10,000 feet) AMSL
across airspace classes A, B, C, D, E, F, and G, the required flight visibility is 8 kilometers, with a cloud
separation of 1,500 meters horizontally and 300 meters (1,000 feet) vertically. For altitudes below 3,050 meters
(10,000 feet) AMSL down to 900 meters (3,000 feet) AMSL (or 300 meters / 1,000 feet above terrain, whichever is higher)
across airspace classes A, B, C, D, E, F, and G, the required flight visibility is 5 kilometers, maintaining 1,500
meters horizontally and 300 meters (1,000 feet) vertically from clouds. For altitudes at and below 900 meters
(3,000 feet) AMSL (or 300 meters / 1,000 feet above terrain, whichever is higher) within airspace classes A, B, C, D,
and E, the visibility requirement is 5 kilometers, with a cloud separation of 1,500 meters horizontally and 300 meters
(1,000 feet) vertically. Within airspace classes F and G at or below this low altitude band, the required visibility is
5 kilometers, and flights must remain clear of cloud with the surface in sight. Additionally, if the transition
altitude is lower than 3,050 meters (10,000 feet) AMSL, Flight Level 100 should replace 10,000 feet. The appropriate
ATS authority may also permit reduced flight visibility down to no less than 1,500 meters for flights operating at
appropriate speeds or in low-traffic/low-level conditions, as well as for helicopters moving at speeds that allow
adequate opportunity to avoid obstacles. Finally, the VMC criteria listed for Class A airspace serve purely as pilot
guidance and do not authorize VFR flights within Class A airspace.

4.2 Unless an air traffic control unit grants clearance, VFR flights are prohibited from landing, taking off,
entering a control zone's traffic zone, or joining a traffic pattern under the following conditions: a) When the cloud
ceiling drops below 450 meters (1,500 feet); or b) When ground visibility falls under 5 kilometers.

4.3 VFR operations conducted between sunset and sunrise—or during any alternative timeframe specified by the relevant
ATS authority—must adhere strictly to conditions established by that authority. Without explicit authorization from the
appropriate ATS authority, VFR flights are forbidden from operating: a) Higher than Flight Level 200; b) At transonic
or supersonic velocities.

4.5 Permits allowing VFR flights to cruise above Flight Level 290 will not be issued in regions where a vertical
separation minimum of 300 meters (1,000 feet) is enforced above Flight Level 290.

4.6 Barring necessary takeoff or landing maneuvers, or formal approval from the competent authority, VFR aircraft
must avoid flying: a) Over crowded districts, towns, settlements, or outdoor public gatherings at an altitude lower
than 300 meters (1,000 feet) above the highest obstacle situated within a 600-meter radius of the aircraft; b) Over
any other terrain or water surface not covered by 4.6 a) at an altitude lower than 150 meters (500 feet).

4.7 Unless overridden by air traffic control instructions or local ATS regulations, VFR level cruising flights
maintained higher than 900 meters (3,000 feet) above the ground, water, or an alternative specified datum must fly
at a cruising altitude that corresponds to their track trajectory based on the tables found in Appendix 3.

4.8 VFR flights must comply with the requirements detailed in section 3.6 under the following conditions: a) When
operating within Airspace Classes B, C, and D; b) When taking part in aerodrome traffic at controlled airports; or
c) When flying as special VFR flights.

4.9 When a VFR flight operates within, into, or along specific areas or routes designated by the relevant ATS
authority under 3.3.1.2 c) or d), it must maintain a continuous listening watch on the appropriate air-ground
voice communication channel and report its position as necessary to the air traffic services unit responsible for
delivering flight information service.

4.10 Any aircraft operating under visual flight rules that intends to transition to instrument flight rules must:
a) Relay the necessary updates to its existing flight plan if one was previously filed; or b) File a flight plan with
the appropriate air traffic services unit—as mandated by 3.3.1.2—and secure clearance prior to continuing under IFR
while in controlled airspace.
